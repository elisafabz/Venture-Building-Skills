# Hypothesis Patterns for Venture Due Diligence

## Core categories

Every venture DD identifies five critical hypotheses. Select from these categories based on the
venture's context, then customise the hypothesis text.

### 1. Desirability (problem-solution fit)

**Default hypothesis:** "The target group has problem X and is actively looking for solutions."

**Sub-patterns by model:**
- **B2C:** "End customers are willing to pay for solution X rather than use free alternatives."
- **B2B:** "Companies have budget for problem X and the decision maker recognises the pain."
- **Marketplace:** "Both sides (supply and demand) have an unsolved pain that brings them to the
  platform."
- **Healthcare / older users:** "The target group (or their relatives and carers) recognises the
  problem and is able to act."

**Test methods:**
- WebSearch for forums, Reddit and Trustpilot reviews of existing solutions
- Google Trends for the search volume of relevant terms
- Existing studies and surveys on how the problem is perceived

### 2. Market size (TAM/SAM/SOM)

**Default hypothesis:** "The addressable market is big enough for a scalable company (TAM above
100 million)."

**Sub-patterns by geography:**
- **Single country:** the national statistics office as the primary source
- **EU-wide:** Eurostat, European Commission studies
- **Global:** World Bank, OECD, industry reports (Statista, McKinsey, CB Insights)

**Test methods:**
- Top-down: total market × relevant share × price point
- Bottom-up: number of potential customers × expected ARPU
- Analogy: comparable markets in other regions or industries

### 3. Competition (the competitive landscape)

**Default hypothesis:** "Existing solutions leave a relevant gap that the venture can fill."

**Sub-patterns:**
- **Direct competition:** "No existing provider solves problem X completely."
- **Indirect competition:** "The current workarounds (spreadsheets, phone, paper) are inefficient
  enough to justify a switch."
- **Substitution:** "The venture offers a 10x better experience, price or speed than existing
  alternatives."

**Test methods:**
- WebSearch for direct competitors and their funding and traction
- Crunchbase and LinkedIn for competitor size and growth
- App store, G2 and Capterra reviews for the weaknesses of existing solutions
- Examine failed predecessors (important — why did they fail?)

### 4. Business model (monetisation and unit economics)

**Default hypothesis:** "The target group's willingness to pay covers the unit economics under
realistic assumptions."

**Sub-patterns by model:**
- **SaaS:** "Customers pay X per month and the LTV:CAC ratio is above 3:1."
- **Marketplace:** "A take rate of X% is standard for this market and covers the acquisition cost
  of both sides."
- **Transactional:** "Transaction volume × margin produces viable unit economics."
- **Freemium:** "The free-to-paid conversion rate is realistic (benchmark: 2-5% B2C, 5-15% B2B)."
- **Regulated or publicly funded:** "Public funding or insurance reimbursement is a realistic
  revenue stream."

**Test methods:**
- Research benchmarks for comparable business models
- Analyse the pricing of existing competitors
- Public financial data of listed comparable companies

### 5. Legal and regulatory

**Default hypothesis:** "There are no regulatory hurdles that fundamentally block the business
model."

**Sub-patterns by domain** (EU-level instruments; each is implemented or supplemented differently
per country, so always verify against national law in the target markets):
- **Health and care:** EU MDR 2017/745 for devices, Directive 2001/83/EC for medicinal products,
  GDPR Art. 9 for health data, plus national rules on regulated professions
- **Finance and insurance:** the relevant EU financial services framework (MiFID II, PSD2, IDD)
  plus the national financial supervisory authority and consumer protection rules
- **Marketplace and gig work:** worker classification, the Platform Work Directive, national
  trade licensing, consumer protection law
- **Care and social services:** national social security and long-term care law, plus the funding
  structures that differ substantially per country
- **General:** GDPR, the E-Commerce Directive 2000/31/EC, the Consumer Rights Directive
  2011/83/EU, the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC

**Test methods:**
- WebSearch for the regulatory requirements in the industry and in each target country
- Check whether licences or permits are required
- Analyse startups that failed for regulatory reasons

## Prioritisation rules

Sort hypotheses by:
1. **Criticality** — would failure kill the venture? (existential > important > nice to have)
2. **Uncertainty** — how much do we NOT know? (unknown > partially known > well understood)
3. **Cost to test** — how expensive is validation? (cheap desk research first, expensive field
   tests last)

Always test the cheapest-to-validate existential risks first.
