# Hook Categories

When testing a new hook, use a DIFFERENT category from the current one. That way you genuinely test different angles rather than variations of the same approach.

## Categories

| Category | Mechanism | Example | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pain point** | Name the pain directly | "Tired of [problem]?" | Products that solve a clear pain |
| **Social Proof** | Numbers, testimonials, authority | "10,000+ already use..." | Established products, trust building |
| **Curiosity gap** | Spark curiosity, leave a question open | "The one thing 90% get wrong..." | Content, newsletters, info products |
| **Contrarian** | Surprising counter-position | "Forget everything you know about X" | Thought leadership, differentiated products |
| **Statistic** | A data-led opener | "87% of [audience] make this mistake" | B2B, data-driven audiences |
| **Before/After** | Show transformation | "From [problem] to [result] in [timeframe]" | Coaching, health, SaaS |
| **Urgency** | Time pressure or scarcity | "Only [X] spots left / until [date]" | Events, limited offers, launches |
| **Question** | A direct question to the audience | "What would change if [outcome]?" | Emotionally charged topics |
| **Pattern interrupt** | A surprising statement | "I did [unexpected thing] and [result]" | Social media, video ads |
| **Empathy** | Show understanding | "I know how it feels when [problem]" | Sensitive topics, older audiences |

## Rules for hook tests

1. **Always test at least three different categories** — not three variations of the same one
2. **Hold everything else constant** — change only the hook; body, CTA and image stay identical
3. **For video ads:** the first three seconds are the hook. The rest of the video stays the same.
4. **For text ads:** the first line of the primary text is the hook.
5. **For landing pages:** the headline is the hook.
6. **For LinkedIn:** the first two lines (before "see more") are the hook.
7. **For email:** the subject line is the hook.
